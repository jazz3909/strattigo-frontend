Why is phosphofructokinase the committed step of glycolysis?
☼
AI tutor

Great question — this comes up a lot in your Unit 3 material. Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1) is called the committed step because it's the first reaction that locks a molecule into glycolysis with no easy way back.

Here's the logic. The very first reaction (catalyzed by hexokinase) produces glucose-6-phosphate — but that molecule can still branch off into other pathways, like the pentose phosphate pathway. So it isn't truly "committed" yet.

PFK-1 changes that. Once it converts fructose-6-phosphate into fructose-1,6-bisphosphate, the molecule has only one real fate: to continue through glycolysis. That's why PFK-1 is also the pathway's main regulatory valve — the cell controls glycolysis mostly by controlling this one enzyme.
